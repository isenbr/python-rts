 1. Smarter target selection — low effort

  Units could prioritize targets based on threat and suitability:

  - Archers attack enemy archers or low-health units first.
  - Swordsmen pursue nearby archers.
  - Units finish weakened enemies instead of spreading damage.
  - Attackers defend themselves before continuing toward the base.

  This would immediately make combat feel more intentional without changing the overall AI structure.

  2. Tactical positioning — low-to-medium effort

  Improve how units behave during fights:

  - Archers maintain distance and retreat when enemies get too close.
  - Swordsmen screen in front of archers.
  - Units spread out instead of occupying the same position.
  - Archers focus fire while swordsmen distribute themselves across nearby threats.

  This is probably the best value-for-effort upgrade.

  3. Squad attacks — medium effort

  Instead of sending every unit out immediately, the AI could:

  - Gather units near its base.
  - Wait until it has a useful composition or army size.
  - Form a squad with swordsmen in front and archers behind.
  - Launch coordinated attack waves.
  - Replace losses before starting another wave.

  This creates recognizable attacks and gives the player meaningful periods to prepare.

  4. Base defense — medium effort

  The enemy currently abandons its base. A defensive system could:

  - Keep a small reserve force near the Crimson Hold.
  - Detect player units approaching the base.
  - Recall nearby attackers when the base is threatened.
  - Produce swordsmen urgently when enemies reach close range.
  - Resume attacking after the danger passes.

  This would stop small groups from exploiting an undefended enemy base.

  5. Strategic production — medium effort

  Replace the random 40% archer choice with decisions based on battlefield conditions:

  - Produce archers against swordsman-heavy armies.
  - Produce swordsmen when the player has many archers.
  - Save essence for expensive archers when appropriate.
  - Favor cheaper units during emergencies.
  - Adjust army composition based on recent losses.

  This works especially well when combined with squads or base defense.

  6. Multiple AI personalities — medium-to-high effort

  Add difficulty or behavior profiles:

  - Aggressive: frequent small attacks.
  - Defensive: builds a large army and guards its base.
  - Balanced: maintains defenders and launches mixed waves.
  - Reactive: counters the player’s current unit composition.

  This adds replayability without requiring new unit types.

  7. Full state-machine AI — highest effort, best foundation

  Give the enemy explicit strategic states:

  Building → Rallying → Attacking
      ↑          ↓          ↓
  Recovering ← Defending ← Threat detected

  Each state controls recruitment, unit assignments, targets, and transitions. This would make future
  additions—difficulty levels, scouting, flanking, or additional buildings—much easier.

  My recommendation is a combined “smart squad AI”:

  - Threat-aware target selection
  - Archer kiting
  - Swordsmen protecting archers
  - Mixed attack waves
  - Two or three permanent base defenders
  - Production that reacts to the player’s army

  That would produce the largest noticeable improvement while staying reasonably compact within the
  current single-file architecture.
